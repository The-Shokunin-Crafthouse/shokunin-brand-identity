01 · ANATOMY

The mark, decomposed

An eleven-cross grid. Four columns, three rows. The top-right cell of the middle row is held empty by intent — the eleventh cross is where it is by design; the twelfth is not there by design. Inter-cell spacing is 56 source units; cross bounding box is 32 source units.

The deliberate detail · 2g

The missing top-right cell of the middle row encodes S and C — the studio's initials — through a single absence. Marked in the anatomy above with a dashed vermilion outline (the outline is for illustration only — it never appears in the mark itself). Read the grid as letterforms and two readings resolve at the same time.

The S. The top row of four traces the top stroke; the middle row of three — stopped short by the absence — traces the middle stroke reversing direction; the bottom row of four closes with the bottom stroke. The S is readable only because the middle row terminates where it does. Fill the absent cell and the middle row becomes a straight horizontal four, and the S collapses back into a generic grid.

The C. Read the rightmost column alone. The cross at row 1 and the cross at row 3 frame the absent cell between them as the open mouth of a letter C, bracket ends top and bottom, opening rightward.

One absence does the work of two letterforms — the middle-stroke reversal of the S and the open mouth of the C. The studio's initials are not stamped on the mark; they are the mark's underlying structure, surfaced only when the grid is read as a letterform.

03 · CLEAR SPACE

One cross-unit on every side

The minimum clear space around the mark equals one cross bounding box — 32 source units at the canonical scale, equivalent to 12.5% of the primary lockup's width on every side. No type, ruling line, or competing graphic may enter that zone. At display sizes (96px+) the studio prefers doubling this — two cross-units of clear space — but one is the floor.

06 · MISUSE

Six things the mark does not do

Every example below is forbidden. The red diagonal indicates the violation. If a surface requires any of these, the surface is wrong — change the surface, not the mark.

Do not stretch

Non-uniform scale destroys the cross proportions and the grid rhythm. Scale uniformly, always.

Do not rotate

The mark is always set true to the page baseline. The grid is the axis; rotation breaks it.

Do not recolor

The mark uses ink and cream only. Shu (vermilion) is the chromatic accent for interactive states — never for the mark itself.

Do not apply effects

No drop shadow, glow, gradient, or filter. The mark is flat by design — depth comes from surface, not from the mark itself.

Do not place on low-contrast ground

Only on the five approved backgrounds. Mid-tone surfaces silence the mark; the variant must always contrast its ground.

Do not fill the absent cell

The missing top-right of row two is the mark's signature. Completing the grid to twelve crosses destroys the deliberate detail — and the mark.
